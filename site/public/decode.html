How the decoder works

For each trial the brain signal has already been turned into a list of numbers, eight per electrode across 110 electrodes. Those describe how much power there was in each frequency range, how strong the gamma bump was and where it peaked, and how the raw voltage moved right after the picture appeared.

The decoder is a straight line through those numbers. Multiply the list by a matrix, add an offset, and out come six values: how fine the stripes were, how fast the texture fell off with frequency, the contrast, how much it pointed one way, and two numbers that together give the phase. A second model of the same shape gives the seven probabilities on the right. The drawing is made from those numbers and nothing else.

The weights were fitted six times, each time leaving a sixth of the pictures out. When you pick a trial, the page uses the weights from the round where that trial's picture was in the held-out part. So the answer on screen is a real prediction, not a replay of something the model already had the answer to.

Watch what it gets right. The kind of pattern and how fine it is come through clearly. Where the stripes sit is mostly wrong, and that is not a bug in the tool. It is the thing this signal barely carries. More on why.
