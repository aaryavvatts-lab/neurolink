Why this matters

A check that the result is about vision

A decoder can be right for the wrong reason. If it were picking up on something in the equipment, or on the patient shifting in bed, the useful electrodes would be scattered anywhere. They are not. They sit in a tight patch at the very back of the head, over the first few stops in the visual system, and contacts further forward sit at chance.

The two patients had grids on opposite sides, different sizes, and different recording speeds. The pattern holds in both.

One caveat on the pictures. Surface positions like these come from lining up a scan taken before surgery with one taken after, and the brain shifts a little once the skull is open. A contact may sit a few millimetres from where it is drawn.
