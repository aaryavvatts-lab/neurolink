How it works

What is happening when you drop a file in

The picture is cropped square, shrunk to 256 by 256 and turned to grey. Your browser runs a Fourier transform on it, which splits the picture into how much of it is coarse and how much is fine, and at which angles. From that it reads off five numbers: the strongest spatial frequency, how much the energy points one way, how fast the power falls off with frequency, the contrast and the average brightness.

Those five numbers go into the model. It was fitted on 210 trials from patient 1, using the same five measurements taken from the pictures they actually saw. Fitting used a split where a picture in the test set never appeared in training, so the accuracy figures are what you would get on a picture the model has not met.

The weakness worth knowing: the training pictures were stripes and grainy textures. A photograph of a face is nothing like those. The model will still give you an answer, and that answer is an extrapolation. Treat it as a rough guide outside the range it learned, and read the warning the tool shows when your picture sits far outside that range.
