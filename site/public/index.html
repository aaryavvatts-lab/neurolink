Intracranial EEG · Visual decoding · OpenNeuro ds005953

Reading vision out of the human brain

Two people had electrode arrays placed directly on their visual cortex. They watched gratings and noise patterns flash by, half a second each. This is what we could recover from the raw voltage — and, just as importantly, what we could not.

The pitch

Embed videos with Meta's DINO. Embed brain signals with a brain foundation model. Align the two latent spaces. Reconstruct what the person was seeing.

What the data actually allows

The stimuli are static gratings and noise, not video. And every off-the-shelf "brain foundation model" is trained on scalp EEG, which cannot represent the frequency band where this signal lives. Both facts became the experiment.

What was measured

The stimulus set, measured from pixels rather than trusted from a label

Every image was characterised by its own Fourier statistics, so the reconstruction target is verifiable rather than assumed. The recovered parameters match the dataset's documentation exactly.

02 — THE GATE

First, does the preprocessing reproduce known physiology?

Before any decoding claim is worth making, the pipeline has to recover an effect that is already established. Hermes et al. showed that gratings drive a narrowband gamma oscillation — a genuine spectral bump around 30–80 Hz — while noise patterns drive broadband elevation with no peak at all. If our preprocessing cannot see that, nothing downstream is trustworthy.

05 — RECONSTRUCTION

Re-rendering what they saw

Rather than hallucinate pixels with a generative model that 210 trials cannot constrain, we decode the stimulus's generative parameters — spatial frequency, noise exponent, contrast, phase — and re-render the image from those numbers. Every pixel below traces back to a decoded value.

07 — ANATOMY

Where the signal lives

A decoder can be right for the wrong reasons. If the information is genuinely visual, the informative electrodes should sit over early visual cortex rather than scatter across the array. Each electrode is coloured by its own cross-validated accuracy when used alone.
